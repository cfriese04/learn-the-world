# Contributing

Corrections are the most useful contribution.

## Reporting an error

Open an issue. Each page has a **Report it on GitHub** link that fills in the page for you. Please include three things:

- the page number (for example `US5.3`) and the sentence that is wrong;
- what is wrong;
- a link to a source that shows the correct version, if you have one.

## Standards for a change

The book follows a few rules. A pull request that changes the text needs to keep to them.

1. **Every factual statement rests on a claim, and every claim names its sources.** Write `[[c-id]]` after the sentence it supports. Ledes, tables, map plates and practice notes count too. Do not add superlatives, extra facts or causal explanations that no cited source supports.
2. **Sources must be ones you opened.** Prefer primary documents in authoritative transcriptions, scholarly work, university and museum pages, and government statistics. Do not cite Wikipedia.
3. **Quotations use the source's exact words.** A scholar's words go in quotation marks only when the source is the scholar's own work. A publisher's blurb, a review, or a textbook's summary of a scholar is not the scholar's words: paraphrase it, and credit whoever wrote it ("as summarised by …"). Quote Bible passages in the NRSVUE and the Qur'an in Abdel Haleem's translation.
4. **Label claims honestly.** Use `consensus`, `approx`, `debated` or `tradition`. A debated claim names scholars on each side and gives each side in its strongest form.
5. **Date anything that can change.** Write "as of [date]" for any legal or political status, and give the date of the source.
6. **Treat every side the same way.** Describe what each president, party or movement said and did, favourable or not, in the same tone. Do not add verdicts of your own.
7. **Use BCE and CE.** Write Bible and Qur'an references as given above. Map coordinates are `[longitude, latitude]`.

After editing a chapter source, rebuild it (see the README) and make sure the build reports **0 errors**.
